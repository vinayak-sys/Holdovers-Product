The challenge

When GPS drops, autonomy gets harder.

Many autonomous UAV workflows depend on satellite positioning. In indoor spaces, dense built-up areas, tunnels, or signal-denied conditions, maintaining reliable navigation becomes a challenge.

⌁

GPS dependence

Satellite navigation may be unavailable, degraded, or unreliable in some environments.

◎

Position uncertainty

Autonomous flight needs a dependable estimate of movement and position.

⌖

Changing environments

Lighting, texture, altitude, and surroundings can affect sensor performance.

▧

Multiple sensors

Useful information must be combined from different sensors and sources.

↗

Real-world validation

Systems need repeated testing to understand limits and improve reliability.

Our approach

Navigation built around the environment.

We're developing a GPS-denied navigation system that combines onboard sensing with autonomous flight control. The aim is to estimate motion and support navigation even when satellite positioning cannot be relied on.

⌖

GPS-independent navigation

Explore alternative ways to estimate movement without depending on GPS alone.

◈

Multi-sensor positioning

Bring together data from LiDAR, optical flow, IMU, and other onboard sensors.

↗

Test and improve

Validate in controlled settings and progressively expand the test conditions.

Built and tested

A prototype, not just a concept.

We've built and tested an initial proof of concept using our own GPS-denied navigation setup. In controlled testing, it demonstrated autonomous flight along a predefined path. The next step is to improve robustness and validate performance across more demanding conditions.

Under the hood

How the system comes together.

Our current development approach combines sensors with flight-control and onboard computing. Each component contributes a different view of the drone's motion or surroundings.

Progress so far

Learning through real testing.

The initial proof of concept has been demonstrated in controlled conditions. We are now working on improving the navigation system and building a stronger validation process.

“We want to build drones that can keep navigating when GPS isn't there to help.”

The focus is on practical engineering: testing, understanding failure cases, and improving the system one iteration at a time.
